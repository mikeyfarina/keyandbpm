D minor chords, relative major and Camelot code (7A)

D minor has 1 flat (B♭). Its chords are Dm, E°, F, Gm, Am, B♭ and C. The relative major is F major and the Camelot code is 7A, so it mixes cleanly with 6A, 8A and 7B.

In practice most songs in D minor swap the minor v for A. Raising the seventh note (C to C♯) gives a leading note that pulls back to D, so A to Dm sounds like arriving home.

Common progressions in D minor

  • Dm – B♭ – F – C (i–VI–III–VII): the minor-key pop and trap loop.
  • Dm – Gm – Am (i–iv–v): the plain minor blues shape.
  • Dm – C – B♭ – A (i–VII–VI–V): the descending Andalusian cadence, with a major V.
  • Dm – Gm – C – F (i–iv–VII–III): a common lo-fi and R&B cycle.

Relative and parallel keys

The relative major, F major, uses exactly the same notes with a different home chord. The parallel major, D major, keeps D as home but changes three notes. More on the difference in relative major and minor keys.

Questions people ask

What chords are in D minor?

Dm (i), E° (ii°), F (III), Gm (iv), Am (v), B♭ (VI) and C (VII).

What is the relative major of D minor?

F major. It uses the same 7 notes, D, E, F, G, A, B♭, C, but centres on F instead of D.

What is the Camelot code for D minor?

7A. In Open Key notation it is 12m.

What keys mix well with D minor?

G minor (6A), A minor (8A) and F major (7B). They sit next to 7A on the Camelot wheel.

How many sharps or flats does D minor have?

1 flat (B♭).
