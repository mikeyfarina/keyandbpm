E major chords, relative minor and Camelot code (12B)

E major has 4 sharps (F♯, C♯, G♯, D♯). Its chords are E, F♯m, G♯m, A, B, C♯m and D♯°. The relative minor is C♯ minor and the Camelot code is 12B, so it mixes cleanly with 11B, 1B and 12A.

Common progressions in E major

  • E – B – C♯m – A (I–V–vi–IV): the most used pop progression of the last forty years.
  • E – C♯m – A – B (I–vi–IV–V): the 1950s doo-wop turnaround.
  • F♯m – B – E (ii–V–I): the jazz cadence.
  • C♯m – A – E – B (vi–IV–I–V): the same four chords as I–V–vi–IV, started on the minor chord for a darker feel.

Relative and parallel keys

The relative minor, C♯ minor, uses exactly the same notes with a different home chord. The parallel minor, E minor, keeps E as home but changes three notes. More on the difference in relative major and minor keys.

Questions people ask

What chords are in E major?

E (I), F♯m (ii), G♯m (iii), A (IV), B (V), C♯m (vi) and D♯° (vii°).

What is the relative minor of E major?

C♯ minor. It uses the same 7 notes, E, F♯, G♯, A, B, C♯, D♯, but centres on C♯ instead of E.

What is the Camelot code for E major?

12B. In Open Key notation it is 5d.

What keys mix well with E major?

A major (11B), B major (1B) and C♯ minor (12A). They sit next to 12B on the Camelot wheel.

How many sharps or flats does E major have?

4 sharps (F♯, C♯, G♯, D♯).
