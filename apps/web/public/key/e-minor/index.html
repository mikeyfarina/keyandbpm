E minor chords, relative major and Camelot code (9A)

E minor has 1 sharp (F♯). Its chords are Em, F♯°, G, Am, Bm, C and D. The relative major is G major and the Camelot code is 9A, so it mixes cleanly with 8A, 10A and 9B.

In practice most songs in E minor swap the minor v for B. Raising the seventh note (D to D♯) gives a leading note that pulls back to E, so B to Em sounds like arriving home.

Common progressions in E minor

  • Em – C – G – D (i–VI–III–VII): the minor-key pop and trap loop.
  • Em – Am – Bm (i–iv–v): the plain minor blues shape.
  • Em – D – C – B (i–VII–VI–V): the descending Andalusian cadence, with a major V.
  • Em – Am – D – G (i–iv–VII–III): a common lo-fi and R&B cycle.

Relative and parallel keys

The relative major, G major, uses exactly the same notes with a different home chord. The parallel major, E major, keeps E as home but changes three notes. More on the difference in relative major and minor keys.

Questions people ask

What chords are in E minor?

Em (i), F♯° (ii°), G (III), Am (iv), Bm (v), C (VI) and D (VII).

What is the relative major of E minor?

G major. It uses the same 7 notes, E, F♯, G, A, B, C, D, but centres on G instead of E.

What is the Camelot code for E minor?

9A. In Open Key notation it is 2m.

What keys mix well with E minor?

A minor (8A), B minor (10A) and G major (9B). They sit next to 9A on the Camelot wheel.

How many sharps or flats does E minor have?

1 sharp (F♯).
