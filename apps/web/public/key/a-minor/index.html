A minor chords, relative major and Camelot code (8A)

A minor has no sharps or flats. Its chords are Am, B°, C, Dm, Em, F and G. The relative major is C major and the Camelot code is 8A, so it mixes cleanly with 7A, 9A and 8B.

Chords in A minor

Stack every other note of the scale on each degree and you get the seven chords that belong to the key:

DegreeChordNotes
iAmA · C · E
ii°B°B · D · F
IIICC · E · G
ivDmD · F · A
vEmE · G · B
VIFF · A · C
VIIGG · B · D

In practice most songs in A minor swap the minor v for E. Raising the seventh note (G to G♯) gives a leading note that pulls back to A, so E to Am sounds like arriving home.

Common progressions in A minor

  • Am – F – C – G (i–VI–III–VII): the minor-key pop and trap loop.
  • Am – Dm – Em (i–iv–v): the plain minor blues shape.
  • Am – G – F – E (i–VII–VI–V): the descending Andalusian cadence, with a major V.
  • Am – Dm – G – C (i–iv–VII–III): a common lo-fi and R&B cycle.

Keys that mix with A minor

Relative and parallel keys

The relative major, C major, uses exactly the same notes with a different home chord. The parallel major, A major, keeps A as home but changes three notes. More on the difference in relative major and minor keys.

Questions people ask

What chords are in A minor?

Am (i), B° (ii°), C (III), Dm (iv), Em (v), F (VI) and G (VII).

What is the relative major of A minor?

C major. It uses the same 7 notes, A, B, C, D, E, F, G, but centres on C instead of A.

What is the Camelot code for A minor?

8A. In Open Key notation it is 1m.

What keys mix well with A minor?

D minor (7A), E minor (9A) and C major (8B). They sit next to 8A on the Camelot wheel.

How many sharps or flats does A minor have?

No sharps or flats.
