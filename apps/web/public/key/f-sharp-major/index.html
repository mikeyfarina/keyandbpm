F# major chords, relative minor and Camelot code (2B)

F♯ major has 6 sharps (F♯, C♯, G♯, D♯, A♯, E♯). Its chords are F♯, G♯m, A♯m, B, C♯, D♯m and E♯°. The relative minor is E♭ minor (D♯ minor) and the Camelot code is 2B, so it mixes cleanly with 1B, 3B and 2A.

Common progressions in F♯ major

  • F♯ – C♯ – D♯m – B (I–V–vi–IV): the most used pop progression of the last forty years.
  • F♯ – D♯m – B – C♯ (I–vi–IV–V): the 1950s doo-wop turnaround.
  • G♯m – C♯ – F♯ (ii–V–I): the jazz cadence.
  • D♯m – B – F♯ – C♯ (vi–IV–I–V): the same four chords as I–V–vi–IV, started on the minor chord for a darker feel.

Relative and parallel keys

The relative minor, E♭ minor, uses exactly the same notes with a different home chord. The parallel minor, F♯ minor, keeps F♯ as home but changes three notes. More on the difference in relative major and minor keys.

Questions people ask

What chords are in F♯ major?

F♯ (I), G♯m (ii), A♯m (iii), B (IV), C♯ (V), D♯m (vi) and E♯° (vii°).

What is the relative minor of F♯ major?

E♭ minor (D♯ minor). It uses the same 7 notes, F♯, G♯, A♯, B, C♯, D♯, E♯, but centres on E♭ instead of F♯.

What is the Camelot code for F♯ major?

2B. In Open Key notation it is 7d.

What keys mix well with F♯ major?

B major (1B), D♭ major (3B) and E♭ minor (2A). They sit next to 2B on the Camelot wheel.

How many sharps or flats does F♯ major have?

6 sharps (F♯, C♯, G♯, D♯, A♯, E♯).
