B minor chords, relative major and Camelot code (10A)

B minor has 2 sharps (F♯, C♯). Its chords are Bm, C♯°, D, Em, F♯m, G and A. The relative major is D major and the Camelot code is 10A, so it mixes cleanly with 9A, 11A and 10B.

In practice most songs in B minor swap the minor v for F♯. Raising the seventh note (A to A♯) gives a leading note that pulls back to B, so F♯ to Bm sounds like arriving home.

Common progressions in B minor

  • Bm – G – D – A (i–VI–III–VII): the minor-key pop and trap loop.
  • Bm – Em – F♯m (i–iv–v): the plain minor blues shape.
  • Bm – A – G – F♯ (i–VII–VI–V): the descending Andalusian cadence, with a major V.
  • Bm – Em – A – D (i–iv–VII–III): a common lo-fi and R&B cycle.

Relative and parallel keys

The relative major, D major, uses exactly the same notes with a different home chord. The parallel major, B major, keeps B as home but changes three notes. More on the difference in relative major and minor keys.

Questions people ask

What chords are in B minor?

Bm (i), C♯° (ii°), D (III), Em (iv), F♯m (v), G (VI) and A (VII).

What is the relative major of B minor?

D major. It uses the same 7 notes, B, C♯, D, E, F♯, G, A, but centres on D instead of B.

What is the Camelot code for B minor?

10A. In Open Key notation it is 3m.

What keys mix well with B minor?

E minor (9A), F♯ minor (11A) and D major (10B). They sit next to 10A on the Camelot wheel.

How many sharps or flats does B minor have?

2 sharps (F♯, C♯).
