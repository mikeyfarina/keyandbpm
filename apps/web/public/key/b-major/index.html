B major chords, relative minor and Camelot code (1B)

B major has 5 sharps (F♯, C♯, G♯, D♯, A♯). Its chords are B, C♯m, D♯m, E, F♯, G♯m and A♯°. The relative minor is G♯ minor and the Camelot code is 1B, so it mixes cleanly with 12B, 2B and 1A.

Common progressions in B major

  • B – F♯ – G♯m – E (I–V–vi–IV): the most used pop progression of the last forty years.
  • B – G♯m – E – F♯ (I–vi–IV–V): the 1950s doo-wop turnaround.
  • C♯m – F♯ – B (ii–V–I): the jazz cadence.
  • G♯m – E – B – F♯ (vi–IV–I–V): the same four chords as I–V–vi–IV, started on the minor chord for a darker feel.

Relative and parallel keys

The relative minor, G♯ minor, uses exactly the same notes with a different home chord. The parallel minor, B minor, keeps B as home but changes three notes. More on the difference in relative major and minor keys.

Questions people ask

What chords are in B major?

B (I), C♯m (ii), D♯m (iii), E (IV), F♯ (V), G♯m (vi) and A♯° (vii°).

What is the relative minor of B major?

G♯ minor. It uses the same 7 notes, B, C♯, D♯, E, F♯, G♯, A♯, but centres on G♯ instead of B.

What is the Camelot code for B major?

1B. In Open Key notation it is 6d.

What keys mix well with B major?

E major (12B), F♯ major (2B) and G♯ minor (1A). They sit next to 1B on the Camelot wheel.

How many sharps or flats does B major have?

5 sharps (F♯, C♯, G♯, D♯, A♯).
