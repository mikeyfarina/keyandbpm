G minor chords, relative major and Camelot code (6A)

G minor has 2 flats (B♭, E♭). Its chords are Gm, A°, B♭, Cm, Dm, E♭ and F. The relative major is B♭ major and the Camelot code is 6A, so it mixes cleanly with 5A, 7A and 6B.

In practice most songs in G minor swap the minor v for D. Raising the seventh note (F to F♯) gives a leading note that pulls back to G, so D to Gm sounds like arriving home.

Common progressions in G minor

  • Gm – E♭ – B♭ – F (i–VI–III–VII): the minor-key pop and trap loop.
  • Gm – Cm – Dm (i–iv–v): the plain minor blues shape.
  • Gm – F – E♭ – D (i–VII–VI–V): the descending Andalusian cadence, with a major V.
  • Gm – Cm – F – B♭ (i–iv–VII–III): a common lo-fi and R&B cycle.

Relative and parallel keys

The relative major, B♭ major, uses exactly the same notes with a different home chord. The parallel major, G major, keeps G as home but changes three notes. More on the difference in relative major and minor keys.

Questions people ask

What chords are in G minor?

Gm (i), A° (ii°), B♭ (III), Cm (iv), Dm (v), E♭ (VI) and F (VII).

What is the relative major of G minor?

B♭ major. It uses the same 7 notes, G, A, B♭, C, D, E♭, F, but centres on B♭ instead of G.

What is the Camelot code for G minor?

6A. In Open Key notation it is 11m.

What keys mix well with G minor?

C minor (5A), D minor (7A) and B♭ major (6B). They sit next to 6A on the Camelot wheel.

How many sharps or flats does G minor have?

2 flats (B♭, E♭).
