F# minor chords, relative major and Camelot code (11A)

F♯ minor has 3 sharps (F♯, C♯, G♯). Its chords are F♯m, G♯°, A, Bm, C♯m, D and E. The relative major is A major and the Camelot code is 11A, so it mixes cleanly with 10A, 12A and 11B.

Notes in the F♯ minor scale

F♯ minor has 3 sharps (F♯, C♯, G♯). It sounds identical to G♭ minor, which is the same set of pitches spelled differently; F♯ minor is the usual spelling.

In practice most songs in F♯ minor swap the minor v for C♯. Raising the seventh note (E to E♯) gives a leading note that pulls back to F♯, so C♯ to F♯m sounds like arriving home.

Common progressions in F♯ minor

  • F♯m – D – A – E (i–VI–III–VII): the minor-key pop and trap loop.
  • F♯m – Bm – C♯m (i–iv–v): the plain minor blues shape.
  • F♯m – E – D – C♯ (i–VII–VI–V): the descending Andalusian cadence, with a major V.
  • F♯m – Bm – E – A (i–iv–VII–III): a common lo-fi and R&B cycle.

Relative and parallel keys

The relative major, A major, uses exactly the same notes with a different home chord. The parallel major, F♯ major, keeps F♯ as home but changes three notes. More on the difference in relative major and minor keys.

Questions people ask

What chords are in F♯ minor?

F♯m (i), G♯° (ii°), A (III), Bm (iv), C♯m (v), D (VI) and E (VII).

What is the relative major of F♯ minor?

A major. It uses the same 7 notes, F♯, G♯, A, B, C♯, D, E, but centres on A instead of F♯.

What is the Camelot code for F♯ minor?

11A. In Open Key notation it is 4m.

What keys mix well with F♯ minor?

B minor (10A), C♯ minor (12A) and A major (11B). They sit next to 11A on the Camelot wheel.

How many sharps or flats does F♯ minor have?

3 sharps (F♯, C♯, G♯).
